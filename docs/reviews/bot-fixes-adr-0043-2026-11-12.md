# ADR 0043 bot fixes: gate results (2026-11-12)

Evidence for [ADR 0043](../decisions/0043-card-aware-bot-baseline-and-fixes.md): the card-aware
bot now plays in every seat it plays, and its seven policy fixes each passed the seed gate and the
variant check. Six of the seven also pass their held-out position; `ownStack`'s held-out position
fails for an unrelated reason (below). The league gate is recorded at the end.

## What changed

- **One bot.** The table's fallback bot (`fallbackDecide`), a live table's heuristic seats and a
  model's fallback (`escalateOrFallback`) all get card knowledge. `local:heuristic` in bot-bench is
  that bot; `local:heuristic-plain` keeps the first sweep's baseline. bot-bench's table opponents
  stay the plain, pre-fix bot so the recorded reference lines keep holding.
- **Bench.** A `bot-led` lineup kind (the production pipeline in bot mode, escalations recorded per
  case), a seed per local and `bot-led` entry, a lineup-level `seeds` list
  ([bot-gate.json](../../bot-bench/lineups/bot-gate.json), eight seeds), and `bot-bench seeds` for
  the gate.
- **Data.** `PermanentView` carries each attacker's defending player (`attacking`,
  `attackingPlaneswalker`). `CardTraits` carries constant pump sizes (`spellPump`,
  `abilitySelfPump`), team pumps with their keywords (`massPump`) and fixed mana output
  (`manaUnits`).
- **Fixes**, each a `PolicyFix` switch in `packages/bot/src/fixes.ts`, all on by default:
  `ownStack`, `neverYield`, `lethalTargets`, `lethalValue`, `castOrder`, `landColors`,
  `attackReserve`. A bot-league arm `fix-<name>` plays each one alone; `fixed` plays them all.

The held-out positions were written after the fixes were tuned and were not tuned against. Two
notes:

- `shock-the-other-threat` is failed by every entry, with or without `ownStack`, for a reason the
  ADR's fixes do not cover: the bot values any opponent's face at a flat 10 LEP, above killing a
  2/1, so it Shocks a player at 31 life. `ownStack` does keep it off the Grizzly Bears the Bolt
  already kills (the plain bot hits them). Left as found: it is a residual for a later fix, not a
  reason to tune the position.
- `rock-before-the-draw` first passed without `castOrder` because Harmonize drew the Islands on
  top of the library and the land drop then paid for Mind Stone. That was an authoring flaw (the
  position did not test what its briefing says), so the library now holds only Gigantosaurus;
  no bot code changed in response.
- `two-home-to-live` came from a PR review finding, not from a bench failure: `attackReserve`
  called attackers back one at a time and stopped when no single recall improved its count, so a
  seat that needed two blockers home to survive sent everything. The fix calls attackers back,
  the biggest cut in the swing first, until the swing is not lethal, and keeps that plan when it
  is worth more than the attack. The position was written and graded before the fix was run on
  it; the bot before the change fails it on every seed (`BOT_POLICY_VERSION` bumped to
  `2026-11-12.adr0043-fixes.2`).

`bot-bench/src/bot-fixes.test.ts` pins each fix to its positions on two gate seeds.

## League gate (decision 3.3)

Each `fix-<name>` arm against `knowledge` (card-aware, no fixes), seats swapped each seed, turn
cap 120, seed `adr0043`. An arm is blocked when its win rate is below 50% and its 95% interval
lies wholly below 50%.

Decided games only: an engine halt (a card the engine does not model, a budget) or a timeout is
not a result and is left out. `knowledge` and each arm see the same decks and seeds.

| ------------------- | ----- | ------- | ------ | -------- | ------ | -------------------- | -------- | ------------ | ---- |

| `fix-neverYield`    | 400   | 340     | 53     | 5        | 1      | 165–175 (1 draw)     | 48.5%    | 43.2–53.8%   | A    |
| `fix-attackReserve` | 400   | 356     | 39     | 4        | 0      | 184–172 (1 draw)     | 51.7%    | 46.5–56.9%   | A    |
| `fix-lethalTargets` | 100   | 89      | 11     | 0        | 0      | 46–43                | 51.7%    | 41.3–62.1%   | B    |

| `fixed` (all)       | 400   | 337     | 59     | 4        | 0      | 171–166              | 50.7%    | 45.4–56.1%   | B    |

No arm is blocked. Only `fix-neverYield` (48.5%) and `fix-landColors` (48.9%) are below 50%,
and both intervals include 50%. None shows a detectable gain either. That is the expected result for fixes aimed at specific positions,
which come up rarely in a whole game, and it is what the gate is for: it rules out a fix that
costs games, and it was never powered to show a small gain.

- The arms ran on frozen copies of the branch so that later commits could not change them mid-run.
  Tree A is before the planeswalker fix in `attackReserve`'s `worth()` (an attack on a
  planeswalker no longer counts as life lost). Tree B includes it. Neither includes the
  multi-attacker recall added after review (`two-home-to-live`); that change is gated by the
  bench positions and `bot-fixes.test.ts`, not by a league run.
- The 100-game arms have intervals about ±10 points wide, so they rule out only a large
  regression. They were cut to 100 to fit the session; 400 games each would narrow them to about
  ±5 points.
- 9–15% of each arm's games halted (12% pooled, 240 of 2000) on an unmodelled card or a budget.
  A halt is the engine refusing to guess (brief §6.3), and it falls on both arms alike because
  the seats are swapped each seed.
- The one error, in `fix-neverYield` (seed `adr0043-197`), was "casting restriction is not met":
  the free-cast legality bug fixed later in this PR (CR 601.3, found by the ADR 0044 census).
  Tree A predates that fix.

## Consequences seen so far

- The fallback bot and a table's bot seats now play the card-aware bot with every fix. One seeded
  test changed (`session-diplomacy.test.ts`: a different game path lets the random human seat
  break a deal it accepted).
- `neverYield` makes a bot holding an instant take more priority windows, as the ADR expected.
